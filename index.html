Teseu is being designed to bring the convenience of a maps app inside large venues, connecting people with destinations, products and useful services.

The experience would adapt to each place: discover a shop, find and buy groceries, or plan your way through an airport. Each deployment needs its own map, operating rules and integrations.

01 / A simple way in

Scan a QR code.
Step into the experience.

The proposed visitor journey starts in the mobile browser. No app download: a QR code at the venue opens its own tailored guide.

  1. 01

    Scan at the venue

    Open the guide from a sign or kiosk. The venue’s information and services would be brought together in one mobile experience.

  2. 02

    Find what you need

    Search for a place, product or service. Choose your destination and confirm your starting point for a map-based route.

  3. 03

    Take the next step

    Explore offers, build a shopping basket or plan a food collection, depending on the services agreed and integrated for that location.

Proposed access journey. This presentation and its sample interactions are not a live venue service.

Shopping centres / Proposed experience

Less wandering.
More finding.

From a specific shop to a place to pause, the aim is to make the centre easier to explore and its services easier to use.

Scan→Search→Find your way→Discover more
Find shops and services, with AI-assisted search

Visitors could search by shop name or describe what they need: “I want a chocolate cake” or “Where can I find a clothing shop?” An AI assistant would suggest relevant places based on information supplied and maintained by the centre and its retailers.

The directory could also include toilets, lifts, stairs, food courts and other registered services. Response quality would depend on the accuracy and coverage of the available information.

Plan a route through the centre

After choosing a destination, visitors would enter or confirm their starting point. An interactive map would show a route through the centre’s corridors, based on approved floor plans and validated connections.

Camera-based directions are also part of the vision, with separate development and on-site positioning tests required.

Look for lost property

The centre could register found items with descriptions and, where appropriate, photos. Visitors could browse the list and follow the centre’s process to confirm ownership and collect an item.

Discover offers and retailer advertising

Retailers could promote products, offers and promotions within the guide. Clearly labelled advertising could create a commercial opportunity, with pricing and any revenue share for the centre agreed through negotiation.

Find a specific product, not just a shop

A future extension could help visitors identify shops offering a particular product. Reliable availability would require retailer system integrations or another agreed process for keeping product information current.

Supermarkets & wholesale stores / Proposed experience

Find it. Scan it.
Make it your checkout.

A proposed combination of indoor product discovery and mobile self-checkout. The aim is to let customers register and pay for purchases using their own phones.

Find→Scan→Pay→Verify
Enter with benefits or as a guest

A QR code on a sign or kiosk would open the guide in the customer’s browser. The proposed options are identification through the retailer’s loyalty programme, using a CPF in Brazil and the authentication process defined by the retailer, or a guest purchase session with a temporary code.

Benefits such as cashback would depend on an integration with an existing retailer programme. Identification, session handling and use of customer data would be agreed with the retailer.

Find products, prices and offers

Customers could search a connected catalogue, consult prices and offers, and find a route to the aisle and location assigned to a product. Camera-based guidance is a further development goal.

Catalogue data, prices, stock and shelf locations would need to be supplied, integrated and kept up to date by the store.

Build a basket using the phone’s camera

Customers would scan existing product barcodes as they pick up items. The virtual basket would list registered products and show the purchase total before checkout. Alternative labelling could also be studied with the retailer.

Choose a conventional checkout or mobile payment

Customers could continue to a conventional checkout under the store’s agreed process, or complete payment in the site using supported integrations, such as Pix, cards and digital wallets, including Apple Pay where available.

Unlike a shared self-service kiosk, each customer would use their own phone. The aim is to avoid the conventional checkout queue when paying online, while following the store’s verification process.

Verify the purchase at the exit

The proposed exit flow would compare the paid purchase with tagged items detected by sensors. A discrepancy could alert staff to carry out a check.

This capability still needs feasibility studies and real-world validation. Tag and sensor technology, reliability, costs and discrepancy procedures would be assessed with the retailer. Scanning a barcode into a basket does not, by itself, enable automatic remote detection of every item at the exit.

Airports / Proposed experience

Your flight.
Your way through.

A connected terminal journey: find a service, follow flight information, collect a meal and make your way towards the gate.

Check your flight→Plan a stop→Find your gate
Navigate the terminal

Passengers would scan a QR code and search the terminal map for check-in desks, boarding gates, baggage reclaim, toilets, shops, restaurants and other services.

Routes would need to reflect real floors, access restrictions, checkpoints and the areas each passenger may enter. Camera-based guidance would require additional development and validation.

Follow flight times, status and gate updates

Passengers could select or enter a flight to see its scheduled time, status and boarding gate, using an authorised, integrated data source. They could then select the gate as a destination.

Gate and time changes would be shown according to the source’s coverage and update capabilities; live flight information is not connected in the current demonstration.

Order a meal and collect it at the counter

Passengers could explore participating restaurants, choose a meal and order through Teseu, then follow the collection instructions and pick it up at the restaurant counter.

This would require agreements with each restaurant operator and integration with available ordering and payment services. No restaurant partnerships or API access are assumed.

Explore flight booking as a future extension

A further possibility is flight search and ticket purchases through authorised airlines or travel partners. Flight selection, payment, ticket issuance and support would need to be scoped with those partners.

Counter collection refers to food orders. Ticket issuance and presentation would follow the airline’s or travel partner’s process.

04 / Beyond the map

Point your camera.
See the way ahead.

The vision includes augmented-reality navigation: directions overlaid on the live camera view, guiding a visitor towards a selected destination.

For this to work, Teseu would need to determine the visitor’s position and orientation accurately. Camera localisation and guidance still need to be developed and tested in real venues.

A few useful answers

Clarity, from the start.

Is Teseu already installed at these venues?

No customer deployments are claimed on this page. The examples shown are illustrative concepts, not partnerships or endorsements.

What is needed for reliable indoor routes?

A production route needs approved floor plans, mapped connections and on-site validation. The starting point may be entered or confirmed by the visitor. Automatic camera localisation is a separate development goal.

Which features can I use in the current demo?

The homepage offers a schematic route interaction. The Metrocentre demo has a four-place sample, search, saved places and links to official information. Camera navigation, payments, automated exit checks, flight updates and restaurant ordering are proposed capabilities, not live services in these demos.

Can we discuss a project outside Brazil?

Yes. We welcome international project conversations. Scope, delivery, pricing, payment currency and supported payment methods are agreed individually.
